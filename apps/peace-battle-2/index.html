Peace-Battle 2

One board. Two rule sets. Both printed on the same 1932 sheet.

Whose game this is

Lizzie Magie patented The Landlord’s Game in 1904 to teach Henry George’s argument about land. She said so herself in the 1924 patent application:

the object of the game is not only to afford amusement to the players, but to illustrate to them how under the present or prevailing system of land tenure, the landlord has an advantage over other enterprises and also how the single tax would discourage land speculation.

The squares here carry her names. She sold the patent to Parker Brothers in November 1935 for $500 and no royalties; Charles Darrow patented Monopoly a month later and was sold to the world as its inventor. She died in obscurity in 1948. Asked how she felt about it, she said that if the idea reached the people shaking the dice, the whole business would not have been in vain.

Where the rules come from

Both rule sets are taken from the printed rules sheet of the 1932 Adgame Company edition, The Landlord’s Game and Prosperity — the numbered rules, the win conditions, the wage scale, the tax offices, the condemnation of the utilities. Its own summary of the two:

The Landlord’s Game shows why our national housekeeping has gone wrong and Prosperity Game shows how to start it right and keep it going right.

Both sources are committed alongside this game at sources/peace-battle-2/: the 1932 rules sheet, and The Woman Inventor Behind “Monopoly”, Monica M. Smith, Smithsonian Lemelson Center, 2015, which is where the patents, the sale and her own words come from.

What is this game’s and not hers. The 1932 board has forty spaces; this one has twenty, so it fits a phone. That shortening forces the rest:
  • One local utility and one railroad, where the sheet has three of each. The monopoly rates that double the charge cannot come up here.
  • One tax office doing the work of the sheet’s three — personal property, land, improvements — charged together.
  • Rule 1(a) doubles land rent on a side of the board once four Improvements stand on it. A side here carries two or three buildable squares, so the threshold is two.
  • Day-laborer’s wages are $100. The sheet names the other five wage rates and not this one.
  • Starting capital is $350. The sheet says $300 in its opening paragraph, deals $350 in its setup table, and prints the Prosperity target as “$7000 (double his original capital)”, which is right for neither. $350 and $700 make it consistent.
She did not patent two rule sets; the patents describe one game. The second set is the 1932 edition’s.
