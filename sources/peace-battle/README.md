# Peace-Battle — source text

The game in `apps/peace-battle/` models one passage from a real book. That book is
committed here in full so every number in the game can be checked against it.

## The book

*The Negro Problem* (1903) — seven essays by Booker T. Washington, W. E. B. Du Bois,
Charles W. Chesnutt, Wilford H. Smith, H. T. Kealing, Paul Laurence Dunbar and
T. Thomas Fortune.

`the-negro-problem-1903.txt` is the Project Gutenberg edition, eBook #15041, released
February 14 2005 and last updated December 14 2020. Public domain in the United States.
Online: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/15041

It is not precached by the service worker and is not part of the offline download. It is
here to be read and checked, not shipped.

## The passage the game models

From Du Bois's essay *The Talented Tenth*, the second in the book:

> they founded colleges, and up from the colleges shot normal schools, and out from the
> normal schools went teachers, and around the normal teachers clustered other teachers
> to teach the public schools; the college trained in Greek and Latin and mathematics,
> 2,000 men; and these men trained full 50,000 others in morals and manners, and they in
> turn taught thrift and the alphabet to nine millions of men, who to-day hold
> $300,000,000 of property. It was a miracle--the most wonderful peace-battle of the 19th
> century

The game's two rates come from those three numbers and nothing else:

- 2,000 to 50,000 is twenty-five times over.
- 50,000 to nine million is a hundred and eighty times over.

The game's name comes from the last line of the passage.

## What the numbers are, and are not

They are Du Bois's, written in 1903 to make an argument. They are not an audit, and this
repository does not present them as one. The game states whose figures they are on screen.

Everything past the second round is arithmetic on those two rates, not a record of
anything that happened. The game marks it as such.
